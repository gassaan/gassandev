# Rebuild Hulhangu's footer around reader trust

Big news sites do not use the footer to decorate the page. They use it as a steady place where readers can check who runs the site, how to reach them and what rules the site follows. Every one of the ten international outlets studied ends each page with a legal and company row: about, privacy, terms, cookies, accessibility, copyright. Most then add one of two things. Sitemap-heavy sites (CNN, Washington Post, Reuters, BBC) repeat their sections. Quality-focused sites (Guardian, Reuters, AP, NPR, Washington Post, Al Jazeera) add trust links such as corrections, ethics codes and news tips. Hulhangu's footer already covers the social and category side well. Its weak spot is the trust side. It has no "About us", no named editor or team, no corrections channel, no registration or publisher line and no way to send a news tip. Those are the pages that Google News, the Trust Project and the Journalism Trust Initiative all say a news site should have. The top recommendation is to add a small trust row and create five or six short pages behind it. Next, add the seven site sections next to the categories, but list "reports" only once, because it is both a section and a category. On phones, keep the footer stacked and fully visible rather than collapsing it behind menus. A warning on the evidence: none of the news sites could be opened live during research. Only the Guardian's and the BBC's footers (including BBC Arabic and Urdu, the only right-to-left examples) were checked against their own source code. The other international footers come from 2025 text copies of their pages. Everything about Maldivian footers (Sun, Mihaaru, Avas, PSM, PNC) comes from search-engine snippets or was not found at all.

Two findings stand out for a small outlet. First, **sites that sell subscriptions use the footer as a shop window**: "Get The Post" has 15 links, and NYT, The Economist and CNN all carry Subscribe and Terms of Sale links. Hulhangu has no paid product, so none of this applies. Second, **trust links are cheap and common at the most respected outlets and missing at the most commercial ones.** Seven of the ten carry some form of ethics, standards or corrections link. CNN, NYT and The Economist show none ([WaPo scrape](https://github.com/BstWPY/WildGraphBench/blob/main/corpus/people/Donald%20Trump/reference_pages/Historians%20just%20ranked%20the%20presidents%20Trump%20wasnt%20last.txt)). Two outlets go further and put a short mission paragraph in the footer itself. AP's reads "an independent global news organization dedicated to factual reporting. Founded in 1846…" and Reuters has a block headed "Information you can trust." A one-sentence Thaana statement of purpose could do the same job for Hulhangu.

Several items appear on almost no footer and have no place on Hulhangu's. **No site put a search box in its footer.** Only three offered app-store links (Reuters, The Economist, WaPo), and each of them has a real app. The long lists of US privacy links ("Do Not Sell or Share", "CA Notice of Collection") exist because of California law. The Guardian keeps "Back to top" to one small button ([BackToTop.tsx](https://github.com/guardian/dotcom-rendering/blob/main/dotcom-rendering/src/components/BackToTop.tsx)). It shows its "Support the Guardian" block only after checking the reader's country and sign-in status ([FooterReaderRevenueLinks](https://github.com/guardian/dotcom-rendering/blob/main/dotcom-rendering/src/components/FooterReaderRevenueLinks.island.tsx)). Big publishers build asks like this carefully rather than crowding them into the footer.

## BBC Arabic shows the right-to-left model; Maldivian evidence is only fragments

The only right-to-left footer checked against its code is the BBC's World Service footer for Arabic and Urdu. It is **deliberately minimal**. It shows a small brand logo (16–24px tall), then a full-width row with a single trust link, "Why you can rely on BBC News". Below that come six or seven institutional links: terms, about, privacy, cookies, contact, other languages, and a consent link that stays in English. The copyright line closes the footer. It has **no social icons, no section lists, no newsletter and no back-to-top button** ([arabic.ts](https://github.com/bbc/simorgh/blob/latest/src/app/lib/config/services/arabic.ts), [urdu.ts](https://github.com/bbc/simorgh/blob/latest/src/app/lib/config/services/urdu.ts)). The design is dark with white text and thin divider lines, and it is capped at 1008px wide ([styles](https://github.com/bbc/simorgh/blob/latest/src/app/components/Footer/index.styles.ts)). The BBC also handles mixed scripts carefully. The "©" symbol sits in its own English-tagged span. The year is filled in automatically each year, followed by the copyright text in Arabic or Urdu. Labels that mix scripts, such as "ملفات الارتباط Cookies", stay inline ([Footer/index.tsx](https://github.com/bbc/simorgh/blob/latest/src/app/components/Footer/index.tsx)). The links sit in a CSS grid, and in a right-to-left page a grid places the first column on the right with no extra code. The trust link spans every column ([List styles](https://github.com/bbc/simorgh/blob/latest/src/app/components/Footer/List/index.styles.ts)).

The Maldivian findings come **entirely from search snippets and are unverified**, but they point the same way. Peer copyright lines appear to be in English and name the legal company. Examples are "Copyright © 2026 Mihaaru. All rights reserved." ([Mihaaru](https://mihaaru.com/register)), "© 2026 PSM News. Public Service Media. All rights reserved" with a full Malé street address and postcode ([PSM News](https://psmnews.mv/)), and "Copyright © 2010-2025 Raajje Television Pvt Ltd" ([Raajje](https://raajje.mv/category/news)). Hulhangu's English copyright line therefore probably matches local practice. Raajje lists **separate newsroom and marketing contacts** on +960 numbers ([Raajje](https://raajje.mv/local/english)). Mihaaru runs a **Telegram channel** ([t.me/mihaarulive](https://t.me/s/mihaarulive)). Avas publishes its registration date (Ministry of Home Affairs, 6 November 2014) on its About page ([Avas About](https://avas.mv/page/about)). Sun belongs to a multi-brand media group ([Sun Media Group](https://en.sun.mv/sun_media_group)). Two Maldivian English outlets publish ethics or corrections channels: Maldives Today has a corrections email ([Maldives Today](https://maldivestoday.com/about)) and Maldives Independent has an "Editorial Conduct" page ([Maldives Independent](https://maldivesindependent.com/editorial-conduct)). Readers in this market have therefore seen these trust links before. It is **not confirmed** whether any Maldivian outlet shows a registration number, editor-in-chief or Viber hotline in its footer, even though many people assume they do.

The regulatory context changed recently, which gives trust links more weight. According to search summaries of the President's Office announcement, the **Maldives Media and Broadcasting Regulation Act (No. 16/2025), ratified 18 September 2025**, dissolved the Maldives Media Council. One commission, the Maldives Media and Broadcasting Commission, now registers print, broadcast and online outlets. It can act against outlets that breach its code of ethics, and no outlet may operate unregistered ([President's Office](https://presidency.gov.mv/Press/Article/34936), [MMBRA summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_Media_and_Broadcasting_Regulation_Act)). The research found **no rule saying what must be displayed on a website**, because the Act's text was unreachable. Any footer wording like "registered with the Maldives Media Council" is out of date.

## Phones get stacked or collapsed footers, never hidden ones

On mobile, the verified sites reflow their links rather than removing them. The Guardian stacks its newsletter block above the links and turns its link columns into a two-by-two grid below tablet width ([Footer.tsx](https://github.com/guardian/dotcom-rendering/blob/main/dotcom-rendering/src/components/Footer.tsx)). BBC Arabic steps down from five columns to **two columns on phones** (240–599px wide), and to a single column only below 240px ([List styles](https://github.com/bbc/simorgh/blob/latest/src/app/components/Footer/List/index.styles.ts)). The Washington Post and Al Jazeera appear to use collapsible groups. WaPo's mobile footer has chevron headings, and each Al Jazeera group has a "Show more" toggle. In both, the legal links stay visible and are never folded away. This comes from the text copies, not screenshots. UX research explains the pattern. NN/g calls the footer a "safety net" and warns: "people still use it and rely upon it, so don't try to hide it." It also notes that mobile accordions save space but "can also cause disorientation and too much scrolling" ([NN/g Footers](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/footers/), [NN/g mobile accordions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-accordions/); both from snippets). NN/g praises "fat" footers for long pages but criticises sites that "stuff every link known to humankind" into them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ia-vs-navigation/)). The rule of thumb that follows is to collapse only when there are four or more long groups, and to keep contact and trust links visible without an extra tap.

The accessibility rules that apply most to a footer come from primary W3C text. Buttons and links must be **at least 24×24 CSS pixels** (WCAG 2.2, 2.5.8, level AA), and 44×44 is the stronger target (2.5.5) ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Sticky bars and cookie banners **must not cover** whatever the reader has moved keyboard focus to (2.4.11) ([W3C 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). Contact and help links must appear **in the same relative order on every page** (3.2.6), and GOV.UK cites this rule when explaining why its help links live in the footer ([W3C 3.2.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/consistent-help.html), [GOV.UK footer](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/footer/)). Icon-only links such as Hulhangu's round social buttons need a spoken name, or screen-reader users hear only "link" ([W3C 2.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context.html)). The page should have one site footer (the `<footer>` element sitting directly inside the page body), and each link group inside it should have its own label ([W3C APG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/landmarks/examples/contentinfo.html), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Reference/Roles/contentinfo_role)). Thaana needs one more safeguard. Phone numbers ("+960 …"), email addresses and @handles inside right-to-left text can visually scramble unless the developer isolates them, using `<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"` ([W3C i18n](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/)). On back-to-top buttons, NN/g advises using one only on pages **longer than about four screens** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/back-to-top/), snippet). Hulhangu's homepage and long story chapters probably qualify.

## Trust pages matter more than footer links for search engines

The trust frameworks agree on what a news site should publish, even though none of them requires the links to sit in the footer. Google News looks for "a mission statement, editorial policies and standards, staff information and bios, non-generic contact information, and organizational-level information like owners and/or funding sources" ([Google News policies](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/6204050?hl=en), snippet). The Journalism Trust Initiative standard (CWA 17493, 2019) asks for a code of ethics, evidence of who ultimately owns the outlet, "the identities and contacts of executives", and revenue sources ([JTI](https://journalismtrustinitiative.org/jti-the-standard/), snippet). The Trust Project's indicators add labels that separate news, opinion and sponsored content, plus "Actionable Feedback" channels where readers can report errors ([Trust Project](https://thetrustproject.org/trust-indicators/), snippet). An older 2017 survey found that **53% of participants said a published list of ethics and corrections policies would increase their trust** ([Santa Clara University](https://www.scu.edu/news-and-events/press-releases/2017/nov-2017/the-trust-project-helps-readers-identify-reliable-news.html)). The footer is simply the conventional place to link these pages, and Baymard's research shows that a group of users consistently looks there for policy information ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/footer-needs-return-shipping-links), e-commerce context, snippet).

The footer links themselves carry little search weight. Google's John Mueller has said sitewide footer links are not given much weight ([SERoundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-footer-sitewide-links-weight-21540.html), older secondary report). The often-quoted claim that footer links pass "15–20%" of ranking power has **no primary source**. The real search value comes from giving Google a clear picture of the organization. Since November 2023 Google has read Organization structured data: name, logo, `sameAs` social profiles, address and contact point ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/11/introducing-organization-markup)). Schema.org also defines news-specific properties (`publishingPrinciples`, `correctionsPolicy`, `ethicsPolicy`, `masthead`, `ownershipFundingInfo`) that point to exactly the pages a trust footer links to ([schema.org news](https://schema.org/docs/news.html)). The research found no evidence that Google Search currently uses these news-specific properties. They mainly help Trust Project–style platforms. Adding them costs little once the pages exist.

## Nine footer changes for Hulhangu, ranked

Hulhangu's current footer already does several things right. It is a single card with the logo and tagline, the six categories are large pill-shaped links that are easy to tap, and the round social buttons match the rest of the site. The gaps are in the trust layer, and each one below is ranked by how much it helps the site's credibility against how much work it takes. The first three matter most. The rest are optional, and each can be added or skipped on its own.

| Priority | Idea | Effort | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add a **trust row**: About us, Our team, Editorial policy, Corrections / Report an error, Advertise | Low (links) + medium (writing the pages) | All ten global sites have an About link, and Google News and JTI expect these pages. This is Hulhangu's biggest gap |
| 2 | Add a **publisher and registration line** above the copyright | Low | Shows who legally runs the site under the 2025 media law; Avas and PSM publish comparable facts |
| 3 | Give every **icon link a name** and make buttons at least 44px | Low (developer task) | Required for screen readers (WCAG 2.4.4) and prevents mistaps (2.5.8) |
| 4 | Add the **seven sections** as a second pill group, listing "reports" only once | Low | Long homepages benefit from a "safety net" of sections; today only categories appear |
| 5 | Add a **"Send us news" tip link** (WhatsApp/Viber number or form) | Low | WaPo, the Guardian and Al Jazeera have tip links; WhatsApp reaches about 19% of people for news globally |
| 6 | Add a **Back to top** round button at the end of the footer | Low | Helps on long homepages and story chapters; the Guardian does this |
| 7 | **Fix the copyright line**: automatic year, legal company name, optionally in Thaana | Low | An out-of-date year looks abandoned; BBC Arabic shows how to mix scripts cleanly |
| 8 | Add **"About our polls" and "About Stories"** notes | Low | Tells readers polls are unscientific and stories are fiction, as the Trust Project's labeling indicator asks |
| 9 | Add **structured data** (NewsMediaOrganization) pointing to the trust pages | Medium (developer task, one time) | Helps Google identify the publisher; costs little once the pages exist |

### Priority 1: a trust row that links to real pages

This change matters most. Below the social buttons, replace the current four links with one row of short Thaana text links in this order: About us, Our team, Editorial policy (code of ethics), Corrections and report an error, Contact, Advertise with us, Privacy, Terms. The row should follow AP's split between a news-values group and a legal group, and BBC Arabic's single prominent trust link. If "code of conduct" currently means rules for reader comments, keep it but rename it clearly ("Comment rules"). Newsroom ethics need their own page, because that is what Al Jazeera's "Code of Ethics", AP's "News Values and Principles" and Dawn's code of ethics ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/code-of-ethics/)) cover. Behind the links, Hulhangu should create these pages, each of which can be a few paragraphs long:

| Page to create | What it should say | Framework it satisfies |
|---|---|---|
| About us | Mission in 2–3 sentences, founding date, who owns the site, registration details | Google News, Trust Project "Best Practices" |
| Our team | Named editor-in-chief and key staff, with a contact route | JTI "identities and contacts of executives", schema.org `masthead` |
| Ownership and funding | Who owns the company, how it earns money (ads, sponsors), a statement of editorial independence | JTI, schema.org `ownershipFundingInfo` |
| Editorial policy / code of ethics | Accuracy, fairness, sources, how news, opinion and sponsored content are labelled | Trust Project, schema.org `ethicsPolicy` |
| Corrections policy and report an error | How mistakes are fixed and marked, plus a simple form or email address | IFCN, WaPo "Request a Correction", schema.org `correctionsPolicy` |
| Contact | Street address, phone, email; separate newsroom and advertising contacts, as Raajje does | Google News "non-generic contact", WCAG 3.2.6 |
| Advertise and sponsored content | Rates contact, and how paid content is labelled | Google News sponsorship disclosure |
| Accessibility (optional) | A short note on font sizes, dark/light mode, contact for problems | Present on all ten global footers |
| Cookies (only if ads or analytics set cookies) | What is stored and why | Present on nine or ten global footers |

### Priority 2 and 7: say who publishes the site, and keep the copyright line current

Add one quiet line above the copyright naming the registered company and its registration with the Maldives Media and Broadcasting Commission (or the earlier ministry registration). Use the registration details exactly as they appear on the official certificate. Do not mention the dissolved Media Council. No rule requiring this line was found. It is a low-cost credibility signal under the new law, not a confirmed obligation. The English copyright line can stay, because Maldivian peers appear to use English ones (search evidence only). It should, however, name the legal company and update its year automatically. A Thaana version, with "©" and the year kept in Latin script as BBC Arabic does, is a matter of taste.

### Priority 3: make the existing buttons usable by everyone

Ask your developer to give each round social button a spoken label, for example "Hulhangu on YouTube". The buttons and pills should be at least 44px tall, and there should be clear spacing between them. Footer text and pill text should reach normal contrast levels (4.5:1) in **both** the dark purple and the light theme, and the soft glow must not wash out the text. Footer text should stay at about 14–16px or larger, because small Thaana is hard to read. That size is a design judgment; no Thaana legibility study was found.

### Priority 4: add sections, but do not repeat "reports"

Put the seven sections (latest, most popular, reports, stories, photo gallery, graphics, polls) above the six categories as a second group of pills, with a small heading for each group. "Reports" appears both as a section and as the category ރިޕޯޓު, so list it once to avoid two identical links. That makes about 12 pills plus 8–10 trust links, within the "scannable" range that NN/g's warning against overstuffed footers implies. It stays nowhere near CNN's roughly 150 links.

### Priorities 5, 6 and 8: small additions that fit Hulhangu's features

A "Send us news" link (to a WhatsApp or Viber number, or a form) turns readers into sources and matches the Trust Project's "Actionable Feedback" indicator. Only link channels the newsroom actually monitors. Add a Telegram or WhatsApp channel icon only if one exists. A round back-to-top button at the very end of the footer matches the site's round icon style. It should not float over the page, which avoids the sticky-element problem in WCAG 2.4.11. If it does float, put it bottom-left for right-to-left pages. That is a mirroring convention, not a sourced rule. Two short explanatory pages, "About our polls" (reader polls are not scientific surveys) and "About Stories" (serialized fiction, not news, rated by readers), protect the site's credibility. They make sure fiction and opt-in polls are never mistaken for reporting.

### Recommended mobile behaviour: stack, don't collapse

Hulhangu's footer will stay short even after these additions, so **do not use accordions**. Keep the single card and stack its contents top to bottom, starting at the right edge as right-to-left text does. Let the pill groups wrap naturally, show the trust links as a two-column grid like BBC Arabic, and keep contact, social and copyright always visible. Collapse the section and category pill groups into tap-to-open headings only if the footer later grows past four long groups. Never put the footer behind an infinite-scroll list, and never add a permanently sticky bottom bar. The suggested order is below. On desktop the same blocks can sit side by side in columns, with the first column on the right.

```
┌──────────────────────────── footer card ────────────────────────────┐
│                          [Logo]  Thaana tagline + 1-line mission    │
│  Sections:   (Latest) (Popular) (Reports) (Stories) (Gallery) ...   │
│  Categories: (Politics) (Business) (Sports) (World) (Living)        │
│  About us · Our team · Editorial policy · Corrections · Contact     │
│  Advertise · Privacy · Terms · Comment rules                        │
│  Send us news: +960 ... (WhatsApp/Viber)                            │
│  (YouTube) (TikTok) (Instagram) (Facebook)            (Back to top) │
│  Published by [Company Pvt Ltd] · Registered with MMBC [no.]        │
│  © 2026 Hulhangu. All rights reserved.                              │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### What to leave out, and three packages to choose from

Leave out a footer search box (no studied site has one), app-store badges (unless Hulhangu ships an app), a newsletter form (unless newsletters are actually sent), US-style "Do Not Sell" privacy links, a sister-brand strip (Hulhangu has no sister brands), long keyword lists added for SEO, and social platforms the team does not post on. The Guardian dropped X from its footer, which shows that the platform list is an editorial choice ([FooterLinks.scala](https://github.com/guardian/frontend/blob/main/common/app/navigation/FooterLinks.scala)).

| Package | Includes | Best if |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Quick trust fix** | Priorities 1, 2, 3, 7 | You want the biggest credibility gain with the least design change |
| **B. Recommended** | A + sections pills (4), tip link (5), back-to-top (6), polls/stories notes (8) | You want a complete footer that fits Hulhangu's card-and-pill style |
| **C. Full** | B + structured data (9), plus a WhatsApp/Telegram channel or newsletter once they exist | You also want to strengthen how Google identifies Hulhangu |

## Conclusion

The research shows that the footer's value to a young outlet comes from the pages it links to, not from how it looks. The global leaders with the strongest reputations put ethics, corrections and tip links in the footer. The Maldives' 2025 media law now makes the question of who publishes a site and to what standards more pressing. Hulhangu's current footer already has a clean layout and good social links. What it lacks are the pages that let a sceptical reader check who is behind the news. Writing those five or six short pages is the real work. Adding the links is the easy part.

Two things remain unknown. No Maldivian footer could be inspected directly, so Hulhangu cannot yet benchmark itself against Sun, Mihaaru, Avas or PSM. Nor could this research confirm what the new commission requires websites to display. When network access allows, the most useful next steps are to open those four sites on a phone and look at their footers, and to read the Act's text (an English translation by the Centre for Law and Democracy exists). Either could change Priority 2 from optional good practice into a legal requirement.
